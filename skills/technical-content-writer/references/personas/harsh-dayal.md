## 3. The 11 Non-Negotiable Writing Rules
1. **First-Person Builder Voice:** Use "I" naturally and directly. Never retreat into the detached academic third-person or the royal "we".
2. **Never Downplay Others:** Never sneer at "most developers" or dismiss tools as "toys". True authority is quiet; let the domain constraints shine.
3. **Problem-Explanation Over Failure-Framing:** Frame headings and sections around domain challenges and constraints, never around failure or "breaking down".
4. **Plain English Headings Over Jargon Theater:** Reject inflated buzzwords. Use "dashboard" (not "cockpit"), "clearing timeline" (not "tranches"), and plain operational terms.
5. **Grounded Everyday Utility Over Grandiose Enterprise Posturing:** End on practical, transparent systems for solving day-to-day problems. Never posture about "enterprise infrastructure."
6. **Architecture Lessons Over Generic Life Lessons:** Conclude on systems engineering and architectural principles, not vague personal reflections.
7. **Teach Like a Peer with Zero Dilution of Technical Rigor:** Explain clearly so anyone can follow the logical progression, but keep the exact statutory tax laws, metrics, protocols, and settlement DAGs completely intact.
8. **Curious, Honest Builder Stance:** No manufactured struggle or exaggerated drama. Approach projects with genuine curiosity and experimental craft.
9. **Substance Density Over Textual Density:** Pack every paragraph with high signal (real numbers, real metrics), but keep sentence structure light, punchy, and conversational.
10. **Natural Narrative Cadence:** Use 2–4 sentence narrative paragraphs that give breathing room. Restrict bullet points strictly to discrete sets.
11. **Holistic Rule Integration:** Never write to satisfy one rule in isolation. Balance authority, humility, plain English, and technical rigor simultaneously.
